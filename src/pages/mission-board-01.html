The Story of Our Mission

A Jubilee Welcome

In Scripture, a jubilee is a time of rejoicing and restoration: an invitation to remember that everything we possess is a gift and to share those gifts with our neighbors. As St. Gregorios Malankara Orthodox Church celebrates its Golden Jubilee, our mission history is also a story of faith becoming action. It begins with children learning to serve and follows the widening circle of parents, teachers, parish ministries, and volunteers who discovered that simple acts of kindness could touch lives far beyond our church walls.

This story is found in the lives of children encouraged by a backpack or a new uniform, in the companionship of an older neighbor, in a meal offered with dignity, and in the quiet generosity of parish families. The projects changed through the years; the purpose remained the same: to share Christ’s love through service.

Planting the Seeds — 2018–2019

Children and families participating in mission service, 2018

The Sunday School Mission Team grew from a conviction that children learn faith not only through lessons but through service. In 2018, children and parents began reaching beyond the parish: preparing food, sending encouragement to students in grief, helping schoolchildren in Pennsylvania and flood-affected Kerala, visiting seniors, and sharing a Thanksgiving table with their neighbors. These early efforts brought the language of mission into ordinary parish life.

By 2019, the team had become more intentional. At the Harvest Festival, it presented a structured invitation for children of all ages to give their time and talents to the Kingdom of God. Six mission ministries helped young volunteers find a place to serve—whether through food, comforting gifts, care for the church, wellness, or Scripture. What had begun with individual service days was becoming a sustained ministry.

Finding New Ways to Serve — 2020–2022

The COVID-19 pandemic tested the team’s commitment. Visits gave way to phone calls and virtual gatherings; service projects moved to porches, Zoom screens, and careful deliveries. Yet the parish continued providing food and warmth, offering gratitude to first responders, supporting families, helping children remain connected to their language and faith, and bringing companionship to seniors who were isolated.

As restrictions eased, these connections became the foundation for renewed in-person service. Children and families responded to the needs of local schools and shelters, to refugees and to those affected by war, and to neighbors who would otherwise be forgotten. Through the pandemic years, the lesson was clear: distance could change how we served, but not why we served.

A Parish-Wide Calling — 2023–2025

In 2023, a broader parish Mission Team emerged. The return of large collaborative projects, along with established school and community partnerships, showed how much could be accomplished when children, parents, clergy, and ministry groups worked together. At the Managing Committee meeting in August, the team presented plans for projects that would engage the larger parish in both local and global needs.

The growing calendar of charitable work created an opportunity to bring these efforts under a more coordinated structure. The idea of a Mission Board took shape in 2024. In 2025, the SGMOC Mission Board was officially formed, joining representatives from different ministries and developing a shared approach to service. It did not replace the dedication of the Sunday School Mission Team; it built upon that foundation so every ministry and parish member could participate.
