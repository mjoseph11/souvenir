Message from the Trustee

From the Trustee’s Desk

St. Gregorios Malankara Orthodox Church, Bensalem

Mr. Thomas Ninan

Trustee
St. Gregorios Malankara Orthodox Church, Bensalem

A church looks one way from the pew and another way from the ledger. From the pew, you see the altar, the candles, and the faces you have known for years. From the ledger, you see the columns. Twenty dollars, fifty, a little more at Christmas, a little less in January, which is how it always goes. And then there is the name that appears every month for eleven years until, one spring, the donation stops. Behind every one of those names was a person who gave what they could, often more than they could. Some moved away. Many have gone home to the Lord. The ledger has not forgotten them, and neither should we. To read the ledger properly, you have to remember where it begins. This Church began in 1976 with no building, no bank account, and no committees. Everything it owned fit into two suitcases, carried in and out every Sunday by our founder, Very Rev. Fr. K. Mathai Corepiscopa, and by a handful of families who came early to set up and stayed behind to pack it all away again. Two suitcases, a few families, and a faith far larger than anything they owned. Those early years were hard. Money was often short, and there were disagreements serious enough to have ended the Church. Instead, those families prayed, forgave one another, and stayed together. Everything that followed was built on that choice.

Slowly, the handful grew, until other parishes in the Diocese began to look to us as an example. No one would call us perfect, but we learned how to endure. We were further shaped by Rev. Fr. Shibu Mathai, now bishop-elect. Achen brought us clarity, discipline, and renewed energy, teaching us to organize, plan, and build with purpose. I did not learn this history from a book; I grew up watching it happen. My father, K. T. Ninan, was the first Trustee of this Church, and I watched him record those early offerings by hand, long before computers or budgets, long before anyone imagined what this place would become. He was not trying to write history. He was simply being faithful with what had been entrusted to him. From a child running through the basement hall to the Trustee writing these words, I have seen nearly every season of this Church’s life. Some of the hands that once carried those suitcases are no longer with us. I carry their memory, as I know many of you do. As we celebrate this Golden Jubilee, I keep returning to four words: My Church, My Responsibility. Words like these are easy to print and easy to forget by February. But fifty years of records tell a simple story. This Church was built by people who took responsibility for it, quietly, faithfully, and at real cost to themselves. To the Clergy who have served us, to the Trustees who kept the accounts honest and the doors open, and to the Secretaries whose records and correspondence hold us together: Thank You. Most of your work goes unseen, but it matters. To every member who has given financially, and to everyone who has volunteered time and labor without expecting recognition: you may have assumed no one noticed. Someone did. Someone wrote it down. Your faithfulness is part of the record, and part of what carried this Church from two suitcases to the beautiful sanctuary we celebrate today. From the pew, you see beauty. From the ledger, you see faithfulness. Fifty years of grace. Fifty years of struggle. Fifty years of forgiveness. Fifty years of generosity. And above all, fifty years of God shaping a Church that is not perfect, but has stayed faithful.

Two suitcases. Fifty years. The story continues, and the next chapter is ours to write.
My Church. My Responsibility.

Thomas Ninan
Trustee
