Through Trial to Renewal

The Reorganization and Formation of St. Gregorios Malankara Orthodox Church (1984–1988)

The establishment of St. Thomas Malankara Orthodox Church in 1976 marked an important chapter in the life of Orthodox Christians in Philadelphia. Under the spiritual leadership of Rev. Fr. K. Mathai (Mathai Achen), the parish grew and its ministries developed. However, the early years were not without challenges. Differences arose among members concerning the leadership and future direction of the parish, and over time these disagreements became increasingly difficult to resolve within the congregation.

Seeking a resolution to the growing differences, the matter was brought before the Diocesan Metropolitan, H.G. Dr. Thomas Mar Makarios. On July 1, 1984, Makarios Thirumeni convened a General Body meeting at the Church of the Savior Cathedral in Philadelphia. At this meeting, the General Body adopted a resolution calling for the dissolution of St. Thomas Orthodox Church of India at Philadelphia, Inc., and requested the Metropolitan to establish two separate congregations to accommodate the differing groups within the parish. Mathai Achen, who had faithfully guided the community since its establishment, opposed the division, holding firmly to the hope that the parish could remain united.

Following the General Body's resolution, on November 1, 1984, Makarios Thirumeni issued two Kalpanas (ecclesiastical orders), Nos. 68/84/par and 69/84/par, formally recognizing two parishes within the American Diocese. Kalpana No. 69/84/par established St. Gregorios Malankara Orthodox Church and appointed Rev. Fr. K. Mathai as its Vicar, while Kalpana No. 68/84/par recognized St. Baselios Malankara Orthodox Church. The majority of the members of the former St. Thomas parish, including Dn. Dr. Paul Varki, continued their spiritual journey under Mathai Achen's leadership as members of St. Gregorios Malankara Orthodox Church.

The reorganization marked a difficult and painful chapter in the life of the community. The Metropolitan's decisions were subsequently challenged through legal proceedings in the Court of Common Pleas of Delaware County, Pennsylvania (Case No. 85-11387). The dispute raised important questions concerning the authority of the Diocesan Metropolitan, parish governance, and the application of the Constitution of the Malankara Orthodox Syrian Church.

During this period, the matter also received the attention of the Holy Episcopal Synod, which met at the Sophia Center Chapel of the Old Seminary in Kottayam from February 23 to 27, 1987. The Holy Synod ratified the actions taken by the Diocesan Metropolitan in organizing the two congregations as St. Gregorios Malankara Orthodox Church and St. Baselios Malankara Orthodox Church. Subsequently, on March 11, 1987, through Kalpana No. 60/87, His Holiness Baselios Mar Thoma Mathews I directed the faithful to abide by the Kalpana of the Diocesan Metropolitan.

A decisive milestone came on December 12, 1988, when the Honorable Judge William R. Toal, Jr., issued a Final Decree ordering the dissolution of St. Thomas Orthodox Church of India in Philadelphia and affirming the formation and incorporation of the two congregations in accordance with the directives of the Holy Episcopal Synod. The Court also directed that the assets and funds of the former parish be distributed between the two congregations. Accordingly, on December 28, 1988, Commonwealth of Pennsylvania, Department of State, dissolved St. Thomas Orthodox Church of India at Philadelphia, Inc. (Corporation No. 701737) The outcome represented a significant victory for the Malankara Orthodox Church in America, affirming the importance of its constitutional framework and the ecclesiastical authority of the Diocesan Metropolitan in matters of parish administration and governance. What began as a painful division thus became a defining moment in the Church's history, establishing the foundation for two distinct parishes to continue their spiritual missions.
