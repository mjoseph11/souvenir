Our Parish History

A decisive moment came during Holy Week in April 1976. At the invitation of then Dn. Dr. Paul Varki, Late Very Rev Fr K Mathai Cor-Episcopa came from New York to serve as the chief celebrant for the Holy Week services of the ecumenical congregation, beginning with the Pesaha Thursday evening service and continuing through Easter Sunday. Approximately sixty Orthodox faithful from the Philadelphia area participated. The presence and pastoral leadership of Mathai Achen made a deep impression on the community and strengthened a growing hope: that Philadelphia might finally have an Orthodox parish of its own, with Mathai Achen as its priest.

That hope could not be realized immediately. Mathai Achen was living and working in New York, and family and professional responsibilities made relocation difficult. Yet the Orthodox faithful in Philadelphia continued to encourage him to move and help establish a parish. In September 1976, after prayerful consideration, Mathai Achen and his family moved permanently to Philadelphia. What had long been a hope was now beginning to take shape.

On October 10, 1976, Mathai Achen convened a general meeting of Orthodox faithful living in Philadelphia. The purpose of the gathering was to consider formally the establishment of an Orthodox parish in Philadelphia. After discussion, the members unanimously adopted two historic resolutions: to organize an Orthodox parish in the City of Brotherly Love under the Malankara Orthodox Church in India, and to name the newly formed parish as St. Thomas Orthodox Church of India at Philadelphia. Mathai Achen became the first Vicar of this newly established parish.

The resolutions were forwarded to His Holiness Baselios Marthoma Mathews I, Catholicos of the East and to H.G. Dr. Thomas Mar Makarios, then Metropolitan of the Bombay Diocese. Because the American Diocese would not be established until 1979, the Orthodox churches and congregations in the United States and Canada were at that time under the Bombay Diocese. A Kalpana was subsequently issued in 1976 recognizing St. Thomas Orthodox Church of India at Philadelphia as a parish of the Malankara Orthodox Church. Thus, the first officially recognized Malankara Orthodox parish in Philadelphia was born.

Around January 24th 1980, the parish was incorporated with the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania as a religious nonprofit organization. Regular Sunday worship was conducted by Mathai Achen at the chapel of the Church of the Savior at 38th and Chestnut Streets in Philadelphia. H.G. Dr. Thomas Mar Makarios later visited and blessed the parish. With membership of more than sixty families, the young parish entered a new period of organized worship, ministry, and growth.

The journey that had begun with small ecumenical gatherings in homes and rented chapels had now reached a defining milestone. Philadelphia's Orthodox faithful now had an officially recognized parish, a Vicar, and a spiritual home of their own. Yet this was only the beginning. The years ahead would bring remarkable growth, new ministries, difficult transitions, and ultimately the emergence of the parish we know today as St. Gregorios Malankara Orthodox Church.
