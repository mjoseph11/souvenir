A First for Our Youth

“Whether therefore ye eat, or drink, or whatsoever ye do, do all to the glory of God." 1 Corinthians 10:31

The Sunday School marked a historic milestone with its first-ever Nuhro Retreat, "The Orthodox Way of Life" (based on John 4:23), from August 18–20, 2017 at Sandy Cove Ministries in Maryland — our first offsite retreat experience, drawing 90 participants including parents, teachers, and children. The weekend featured teachings from Rev. Fr. Abey George, Rev. Fr. Alexander Kurien, Rev. Dn. Bobby Varghese, Rev. Dn. Saju Varghese, and Rev. Fr. Shibu Mathai, and combined worship (Evening and Morning Prayers, Confession, and Holy Liturgy) with themed workshops for separate parent, youth, and children's groups, plus outdoor activities like ziplining, swimming, fishing, and a riverside bonfire. A particular highlight was Evening Prayer held under a lakeside gazebo with a stunning sunset backdrop, whose beauty and serenity left everyone deeply inspired and spiritually uplifted. Offering students a time of fellowship, reflection, and spiritual renewal, the retreat set the tone for similar retreats going forward.

A New Sunday School Mission Team Takes Root

"As each has received a gift, use it to serve one another, as good stewards of God's varied grace." (1 Peter 4:10)

Mission and charity work at SGMOC Sunday School has always come from the heart — driven by volunteers eager to serve. As the program grew, so did the opportunity to bring even more structure and stewardship to that generosity. The impulse to serve was always there; the structure around it wasn't.

Recognizing an opportunity to build on that spirit, a dedicated Sunday School Mission Team was created. For the first time, all Sunday school mission and charity projects had a formal home — a team responsible for planning, coordinating, and being accountable for the resources entrusted to them. Mission Statement – “to serve through projects that involve and benefit children, staying rooted in the community we serve”

The team's first initiative, the Kerala Backpack Project, was launched in response to the catastrophic 2018 Kerala floods, which displaced millions and devastated communities across the state, including families connected to our own congregation. The Mission Team rallied to collect and distribute 300 backpacks filled with essential school materials for students whose education had been disrupted by the disaster — a powerful early expression of faith lived out through service. This act of service was appreciated by His Holiness Baselios Marthoma Paulose II, who sent a video message of appreciation.

Many other projects like Rise Against Hunger, Thanksgiving meals at local schools, Christmas Caroling and Crafts at a Nursing Home became annual activities. Recognizing that a child's growth extends beyond the classroom, the team conducted a variety of religious and wellness seminars aimed at supporting the spiritual and mental well-being of our children — reflecting a commitment to caring for the whole child: mind, body, and spirit.

What started as a Sunday School initiative didn't stay small. The groundwork laid by this team — the structure, the accountability, the focus — became the foundation for something bigger: the SGMOC Mission Board, now established at the parish level.

Gregorian Harmony: Voices Raised in Worship

“Serve the Lord with gladness: come before his presence with singing." – Psalms 100:2

Sunday School teachers came together to form Gregorian Harmony, a music team dedicated to praising the Lord through song. What began as a shared love for music among teachers soon grew into a ministry of its own — the team lent their voices to various parish and area-level events, filling each occasion with worship and joy.
