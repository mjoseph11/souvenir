The Fifty-Year Journey of St. Gregorios Malankara Orthodox Church, 1976–2026

The Seeds of Faith

Early Malayalee Christian Fellowship in Philadelphia

The 1960s saw the arrival of a growing number of Christians from Kerala, India, to the Philadelphia area for higher education, employment, and other professional opportunities. They represented several Christian traditions, including the Orthodox, Jacobite, Catholic, Mar Thoma, CSI, and Knanaya Churches. Although they came from different denominations, they shared a common language, culture, and faith in Jesus Christ. At the time, none of these communities had a large enough presence in Philadelphia to maintain its own parish and resident priest. As a result, Malayalee Christians came together as an ecumenical fellowship, gathering in one another’s homes for prayer and fellowship and welcoming visiting priests and bishops to conduct worship services.

A significant milestone came in July 1962, when the first recorded Malayalam liturgical service for the Malayalee Christian community in Philadelphia was celebrated in the living room of Late Dr. T. V. Philip, one of the pioneer members of our church, at his apartment at 4900 Pine Street, Philadelphia. Rev. Fr. K. M. Simon of the Knanaya Church was the celebrant. The historical record notes that Dr. Philip himself served as the acolyte for that first service. Beginning in August 1962, regular services were held on the first Sunday of each month at the chapel of Asbury Methodist Church on Chestnut Street.

Late Rev. Fr. Dr. Paul Varki, who was a deacon during those early years and had come to the United States for higher studies, later became a coordinator of the ecumenical congregation. He helped arrange services by contacting available priests in Philadelphia and neighboring areas and inviting them to celebrate the Holy Qurbana and lead the faithful in worship. As the number of faithful increased, the congregation eventually moved from the chapel at Asbury Methodist Church to the chapel of the Episcopal Church of the Savior at 38th and Chestnut Streets in Philadelphia.

This fellowship had no formal denominational affiliation and was not an incorporated parish. It was, rather, a community of Malayalee Christians brought together by a shared desire to worship and remain rooted in their faith while building new lives far from their homeland. These humble gatherings planted the seeds from which an organized Orthodox Christian community in Philadelphia would soon emerge.

From Fellowship to Parish

The Birth of the First Orthodox Parish in Philadelphia - 1976

The ecumenical fellowship of the 1960s and early 1970s had nurtured the faith of Malayalee Christians in Philadelphia, but for the Orthodox faithful, the desire for a parish of their own continued to grow. Until 1976, there was no officially established Malankara Orthodox parish or resident Orthodox priest in Philadelphia. The American Diocese itself had not yet been formed; Orthodox parishes in the United States and Canada were then under the jurisdiction of the Bombay Diocese.
