Our Parish History

In Search of a Home

Faith and Perseverance on the Journey - 1984-1995

Despite the challenges surrounding the parish's reorganization, the faithful of St. Gregorios Malankara Orthodox Church continued their journey with determination and hope. Although the parish had a new identity and a committed congregation, the dream of having a permanent church home remained unfulfilled. After briefly worshipping at a Presbyterian church in Lansdowne, Pennsylvania, the parish returned to the familiar chapel of the Church of the Savior at 38th and Chestnut Streets in Philadelphia. There, the congregation continued to worship for years, holding firmly to the hope that one day it would have a church of its own.

Life without a permanent home brought many practical challenges. Church supplies and items needed for worship had to be transported from place to place, often carried in suitcases and set up wherever services were held. The lack of dedicated space also made it difficult to conduct Sunday School, ministry programs, meetings, and other spiritual activities. Yet these limitations did not weaken the parish. Through persistence, sacrifice, and faith, the congregation continued to worship, serve, and grow.

As parish membership increased, the need for a permanent home became more urgent. Changes in the management of the Church of the Savior also placed increasing pressure on SGMOC to find another location. The dream of having a church of its own was no longer simply a desire; it had become essential for the continued growth of the parish and its ministries.

In 1994, that dream appeared to be within reach when SGMOC purchased approximately 1.5 acres of land with a house at 9897 Verree Road in Philadelphia. The parish hoped to construct a new church on the property and moved forward with extensive preparations. The Church Building Committee worked diligently on the project, architectural plans were prepared, required approvals and permits were pursued, and a construction company was engaged. In the meantime, Holy Qurbana and other services continued at the property.

After so much preparation and anticipation, however, the parish encountered serious zoning difficulties. The planned construction was halted just as work was expected to begin. Although the parish appealed the decision and continued its efforts to move the project forward, permission to construct the proposed church was ultimately not obtained. It was a painful disappointment after years of prayer, planning, and hard work.

Still, the setback at Verree Road did not end the parish's search. What seemed at the time to be a closed door would soon lead the faithful to another opportunity—one that would finally give St. Gregorios Malankara Orthodox Church a place it could call its own.

A Place to Call Our Own

The Torresdale Avenue Years - 1995-2008

In 1995, soon after the disappointment at Verree Road, SGMOC purchased a small church building and its surrounding property at 7721–31 Torresdale Avenue in Philadelphia. The property had already been used as a religious institution and offered something the parish had sought for nearly two decades: a dedicated place of worship. With the sale of the Verree Road property and the purchase of the Torresdale Avenue church, the dream of having a church of its own had finally become a reality after years of worshipping in borrowed and rented spaces.

The new church was dedicated for Orthodox worship by H.G. Thomas Mar Thimothios, then Catholicos-designate, later became the seventh Catholicos of the East, H.H Moran Mar Baselios Marthoma Didymos I, assisted by H.G. Mathews Mar Barnabas, Metropolitan of the American Diocese, and H.G. Dr. Thomas Mar Makarios, Metropolitan of Canada, USA and Europe. For the members who had carried the parish through years of uncertainty and transition, the dedication marked a joyful and deeply meaningful milestone in their pilgrimage.
