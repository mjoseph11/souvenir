Nasrani History

From St. Thomas to Syrians

By Neil Mathew

When most people think of India and religion, they immediately think of Hinduism, or maybe to a lesser extent Sikhism or Islam. Very few people would even consider Christianity, and if they do, they assume it is a holdover of European colonialism. However, this couldn’t be farther from the truth; Christianity is one of India's oldest continuous religions, dating back to the Apostolic Age. It in fact predated European Christianity, existing centuries before Constantine’s conversion. As such, it is important to examine the inception and growth of Christianity in India, and how it led to the current state of the Malankara Orthodox faith.

St. Thomas in India

Tradition holds that Christianity first reached India in 52 A.D. by St. Thomas the Apostle, who landed in Cranganore. It was here that he first started preaching primarily to Brahmins and Jews (many trader and merchant settlements were established by Jews), and established 7 churches, known as the Ezharappallikal. This is further corroborated by the Church Fathers, particularly St. Eusebius of Caesarea, who stated that when St. Pantaenus, a prominent Alexandrian theologian, arrived in Kerala in 190 A.D., he found the Christians using a version of the Gospel of Matthew that included both Hebrew and Syriac wording. The inclusion of Syriac wording is particularly interesting, as it is a dialect of Aramaic, the language Jesus and his Disciples would have spoken.

Growth under Church of the East

Over the next few centuries, the Church in Kerala benefited from the large-scale trade between the Malabar Coast and the Middle East, as many Syrian Christian traders would end up settling in the region. Over time, particularly during the Medieval period, the Church in Kerala coalesced under the Church of the East, a Persian Nestorian Church, who would provide structure and theological guidance. In this system, the Church in Kerala benefited from autonomy and self-governance, but derived its theology and liturgy from the Church of the East.
