Our Parish History

The Bensalem project quickly became a shared undertaking of the entire parish. Members volunteered their time and labor over many weeks to clear and prepare the property. Yet the parish's vision extended beyond constructing a building. Even before moving to Bensalem, SGMOC sought to establish a relationship with its new neighbors. On August, 2007, the church organized its first community event at the property, Community Awareness Day. The event was warmly received, with many neighbors and members of the surrounding community participating. It marked an early expression of the parish's desire to become not simply a church located in Bensalem, but an active part of the Bensalem community.

Another historic milestone came on May 27, 2007, when the parish broke ground for its future church under the leadership of its Vicar, Late Very Rev. K. Mathai Cor-Episcopa. For a congregation whose pilgrimage had taken it through homes, borrowed chapels, and rented facilities, the groundbreaking represented far more than the beginning of a construction project. It was the visible beginning of a long-awaited dream.

On March 30, 2008, under the prayerful guidance of Late H.G. Mathews Mar Barnabas, then Metropolitan of the American Diocese, the foundation stone for the new church was laid. Construction moved forward through the dedication of many selfless individuals and the collective prayers, sacrifices, hard work, and generosity of parish families. What had once seemed a distant hope gradually rose from the Bensalem property as a new spiritual home.

By the end of 2008, the long-cherished dream had become a reality. On Christmas Day, December 25, 2008, the parish began worshipping at its new home in Bensalem. The occasion marked the culmination of decades of searching and perseverance—from carrying worship supplies between temporary locations to finally gathering for Holy Qurbana in a church built through the faith and sacrifice of the community.
